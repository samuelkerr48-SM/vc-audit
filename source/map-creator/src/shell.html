How it works

  1. Add your plans. The tool finds the teaching and learning units in each file:
    • Yearly overviews and scopes and sequences in Excel or Word: a column headed Unit, Topic or Focus gives one unit per row. Term and level come from the rows, columns or tab names (“Term 2”, “Year 4”).
    • Curriculum area plans (weeks across the top, a row per level and semester, as in VCAA’s template): each unit in the grid. Calendar items such as camp or NAPLAN are left out.
    • Headed documents: a section under each heading such as “Weeks 1–3: Fractions” or “Unit 4”.
    • Unit plans are read as one unit. With an overview, a unit plan that matches an overview unit (by unit number, such as 3.4, or by title) adds its detail to that unit.
    Change how a file is read under Read it as.
  2. Check the map. For each unit, a content description is ticked when its VC2 code is written in the plan, or when the plan’s words clearly match it. Where a plan lists codes, its words alone don’t add ticks. Weaker matches show as ?: tick the ones you agree with. Click any cell to tick, clear or change the achievement standard.
  3. The achievement standard number in each cell comes from your template’s list (N1, A7, Sp13 …). It is the extract VCAA links to that content description; where several are linked, the one whose words the unit shares most.
  4. Download your template, filled in. Nothing is uploaded: files are read in this browser.

Every learning area has a built-in template in the same layout as the Mathematics one: a tab per level (or band of levels, such as Levels 3 and 4 for Science and the Humanities), and the achievement standard numbered sentence by sentence and labelled by strand or mode (SL, RV and W for English; SU, SI and SHE for Science). Choose a learning area other than Mathematics or English from Other learning areas. Mathematics and English match plans on curriculum concepts and VCAA’s wording; the other learning areas on VCAA’s wording and elaborations, so check their possible matches. Use your own template (same layout: VC2 codes across, Unit Title and Semester, the AS key below) with “use a different template”.
